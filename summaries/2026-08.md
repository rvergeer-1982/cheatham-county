### Cheatham County — Board of Education

**Sources:** August 4 regular meeting (real minutes obtained 2026-09-11 — see update note below), and August 27 work session (**agenda only, minutes pending**).

**Update 2026-09-11:** The August 4 "minutes" file was originally unreadable (broken BOEconnect viewer file, described below in the original caveat). A real PDF has since been obtained; confirmed outcomes are summarized below in place of the agenda-only account.

**August 4, 2026 (Regular meeting) — now readable:**
- **This was John Patrick's and Robert Epps's final meeting** — Patrick had served since 2020, Epps since 2022. Both were given a commemorative photo and a Riverview Restaurant gift certificate. (This corroborates the County Commission's July 20 minutes, where outgoing 1st District board member John Patrick told commissioners he'd "observed no evidence of fraud or lack of transparency" during his time on the Board — consistent with him departing this meeting.)
- SIP Goal Update and Five Year Plan were confirmed removed from the agenda in revision — no presentation given, matching what the agenda-only record already showed.
- Consent agenda approved 6-0: July 9 minutes; no surplus items this month.
- Approved (6-0 each): United Way Raise Your Hand Grant, $44,000.00; Perkins Reserve Grant, $50,000.00; Preschool Access for All Network, $30,000.00 (FY27).
- **Transportation Bus Request — a real, internal dollar discrepancy inside the BOE's own minutes:** the agenda-revision motion (page 1) approves changing the item's amount to "$1,065,332.00," but the item itself and its final approval motion (page 3) both state "$1,065,322.00." The board's own minutes contain both figures for the same request, $10 apart. The higher figure ($1,065,332.00) matches the County Commission's August 17 agenda for the same seven-bus purchase — so the mismatch flagged last month between the two governments' documents traces back to an inconsistency already present within the Board of Education's own minutes, not two independently-typo'd documents.
- **A genuine vote-count anomaly:** a motion by John Patrick to split two specific policies (4.301 Interscholastic Athletics and 6.303 Questioning Students and Searches) out for individual votes is recorded as "**Motion failed**," immediately followed by a roll call showing all six members voting **Yes** (Yes: 6, No: 0). A unanimous "yes" roll call paired with a "failed" result is a direct contradiction in the document as written — worth asking the board secretary to clarify which is correct.
- Approved 6-0 on **emergency first reading** (bypassing the normal two-reading process): a batch of 23 policy revisions, plus a newly-added 24th policy (Bereavement Leave, added in revision, raising bereavement leave from 2 to 3 days). Substantive changes worth flagging individually:
  - **Interscholastic Athletics (4.301)** and **Questioning Students and Searches (6.303)** both add identical new language defining sex as "the immutable characteristics of the person's reproductive system that identify the person as male or female, as determined by anatomy and genetics existing at the time of birth," tying athletic eligibility to a student's original birth certificate.
  - **Application and Employment (5.106)** adds an E-Verify requirement for all new hires and removes a prior reference to the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986.
  - **Attendance (6.200)** removes the prior "Military Service of Parent/Guardian" section, which had guaranteed a one-day excused absence before/after a parent's deployment and up to 10 excused absences per year to visit a deployed parent — this appears to be a real rollback of a benefit for military families, not a wording cleanup, though no reason is given in the document.
  - Two brand-new policies were adopted: **4.215 Instructional Use of Digital Devices** (limits digital-device use in K-5 classrooms, requires in-person teacher-led instruction as the primary mode) and **6.4053 Outside Applied Behavior Analysis Therapy** (governs private ABA providers working with students on campus).
  - **Student Assignment (6.205)** adds a requirement to separate an alleged child-sexual-abuse victim from an alleged perpetrator within the same school, where substantiated by DCS, a court, or criminal charges.

**August 27, 2026 (Work session):** Still agenda-only, no minutes found. Planned discussion covered insurance premiums and grant carryovers (Title I-A/I-D/II-A/III/IV, IDEA B/PS, a $250,000 safety-camera Ed Debt item, and several six-figure carryover balances), plus Strategic Plan, CCCHS Track, custodial, and ADM/virtual-enrollment updates.

**Flags / issues:**
- The two internal document contradictions above (the $10 bus-financing discrepancy and the "motion failed" vs. unanimous "yes" vote) are both errors within the Board of Education's own official minutes, not disagreements between different government bodies — worth flagging to the board directly since they affect the accuracy of the public record.
- No reason is given anywhere in the minutes for rolling back the military-family attendance accommodations — worth asking about directly rather than assuming intent.
- One dollar figure in the August 27 agenda is oddly formatted: "Title IA $15,1547.23" has an extra digit and doesn't parse as a normal currency amount — likely a source-document typo.

### Ashland City — Mayor and Council

**Sources:** August 4 (Workshop meeting — approved minutes) and August 11 (Regular Council meeting — approved minutes). Reading them together shows the two-step process this council uses: workshop discussion first, formal vote the following week.

**What happened:**
- At the August 4 workshop, department reports were given (Attorney, Codes, City Recorder, Court, Finance, Fire, HR, Parks Board, Parks Dept., Police, Public Works, Technology, Thrive 55+), and the council **discussed** (did not vote on) several items later voted on August 11: a new city seal/challenge coin, an ordinance amending zoning for vape shops (2nd reading), an ordinance rezoning "Natures Trail" (2nd reading), a fireworks ordinance amendment (2nd reading), a website vendor, "Bruce's Buddies" backpacks, repeat codes-violation offenders, a WWTP fire monitoring agreement, a GNRC fiscal policies resolution, and a first-reading FY27 budget amendment.
- At the August 11 regular meeting, the council formally voted on nearly all of the above:
  - **New City Seal and Challenge Coin** — approved 6–1, with Councilman Kerrigan the lone "nay." This is the only non-unanimous vote across both Ashland City council meetings this month.
  - Vape shop zoning ordinance — approved unanimously.
  - Rezone of "Natures Trail" — approved unanimously; a public hearing was opened on this item and no one spoke.
  - Fireworks ordinance amendment — approved unanimously.
  - Website vendor agreement with CivicPlus — approved unanimously, contingent on City Attorney review.
  - WWTP Fire Monitoring Agreement — approved unanimously.
  - GNRC Fiscal Policies and Procedures Manual (Resolution 2026-44) — approved unanimously, with a change to the heading.
  - Budget Amendment #1, FY27 (Ordinance 653) — approved unanimously on first reading.
  - **"Bruce's Buddies" backpack discussion — tabled** (unanimous).
  - **Codes violation repeat-offenders discussion — deferred.**
  - **Parks Board discussion — deferred** (this item had been added to the agenda specifically by the Mayor on August 4).
- Two public forum speakers (Vicki Canon, Dennis LaMotte) are named at the August 11 meeting, but the minutes don't record what either said. The Mayor separately noted he'd like to add Mr. LaMotte to the September 1 meeting.

**Flags / issues:**
- The city seal/challenge coin vote is the only split vote in either Ashland City meeting this month — worth knowing that's the one item where a council member publicly dissented.
- Three items — Bruce's Buddies backpacks, codes violation repeat offenders, and the Parks Board discussion — were all punted (tabled or deferred) at the same meeting with no reasons recorded. Bruce's Buddies in particular went from "discussed" on August 4 to "tabled" on August 11 without any visible resolution — a recurring topic that keeps getting pushed off.
- Public forum speakers are named but their remarks are not summarized in the minutes, and it's unclear from this record whether Mr. LaMotte's comment prompted the "add to September meeting" note or was unrelated.

### Cross-board notes

- **Dollar mismatch on the school-bus financing, now traced to its source:** The Cheatham County Commission's August 17 agenda lists an Interfund Capital Outlay Note "not to exceed $1,065,332.00" for seven school buses. The Board of Education's own August 4 minutes (obtained 2026-09-11) show both $1,065,332.00 (in the motion to revise the agenda) and $1,065,322.00 (in the actual line item and final approval) for the same request — see Board of Education section. The County Commission's figure matches the higher of the two BOE numbers, meaning the $10 discrepancy originates inside the Board of Education's own minutes, not from two separately-typo'd government documents.
- **Harpeth High School construction ties county and town government together:** The Cheatham County Commission's August 17 agenda includes an informational item for a debt note up to $7,000,000 for a "Maintenance/Tech Building and HHS Construction Technical Career Path Building." The same month, both the Kingston Springs Design Review Committee and the Kingston Springs Planning Commission (both meeting August 13) have agenda items reviewing site plans and building materials for the "Harpeth High School CTE (Career and Technical Education) Training Building" at 170 E Kingston Springs Rd. These appear to be the same school construction project moving through county financing and town land-use review in the same month — worth tracking as one story across both jurisdictions once all the relevant minutes are posted.
- **Ashland City's "Parks Board" thread:** Ashland City Council added a "Parks Board Discussion" to its August 4 agenda, then deferred it without discussion on August 11. The Ashland City Parks Advisory Board's own August 25 agenda separately lists a "Parks Master Plan" as new business. It's not confirmed from these documents whether the two are the same underlying issue, but the timing and subject overlap are worth watching together.
- **Kingston Springs Design Review Committee and Planning Commission met the same day (August 13) with a coordinated agenda** — the Planning Commission's agenda explicitly plans to recess for the Design Review Committee's recommendation on the Harpeth High School project, then reconvene. This is a normal, disclosed procedural link between the two bodies, not a red flag on its own — noted here because it's the clearest example this month of two boards' business being directly linked.
- **Transparency gap, county-wide (as of the 2026-09-09 download):** Of the 16 source documents originally reviewed, **8 meetings had no approved minutes posted**: Cheatham County Commission (Aug 17), Cheatham County Election Commission (Aug 20), Cheatham County Board of Education work session (Aug 27), and all four Kingston Springs bodies (Beer Board, Board of Commissioners, Design Review Committee, Planning Commission — all Aug 13 or Aug 20).
