### Cheatham County — County Commission

**Source:** approved minutes, August 17, 2026 (70-page signed PDF with all 24 resolutions attached). Pulled 2026-10-07. This replaces the agenda-only write-up from the September pull.

**What happened:**
- All 12 commissioners present. Chairman Tim Williamson presided.
- Recognized three outgoing commissioners: Mike Breedlove, Eugene O. Evans Sr., Bill Powers. County Mayor Kerry McCarver is also leaving after 9 years and declined a recognition.
- **Both top finance staff resigned.** The Mayor announced the resignations of Director of Accounts Sandrine Batts and Assistant Director of Accounts Mary Kathryn Deason. No reason or effective date is recorded.
- Zone change E-1 to R-1 for Timothy and Wanda Baker (Split Rail Cir., 6th District) approved 12–0. No one spoke at the public hearing.
- Budget amendments, all approved 12–0:
  - General Fund, $38,739.54 total: $20,000 from the Alcohol and Drug Treatment Reserve for D.A.R.E. supplies; $841.54 for a temporary Veterans Service employee; $420 courtroom security; $4,000 AO Smith donation for the Sheriff's Christmas event; $13,478 from fund balance to fix two payroll lines "inadvertently budgeted at the incorrect salaries" (Probation $10,712, Property Assessor $2,766).
  - **$322,875 from General Fund balance for architect fees on the new South Cheatham Library in Kingston Springs** (deferred from July).
  - $35,607.54 of Sports Gaming Privilege Tax reserve moved to the Highway Department for paving.
  - Vehicles fund: $44,015 insurance recovery on a totaled patrol car, and a $305,461 federal-through-state grant for one ambulance.
  - $6,600 of opioid settlement money to Hustle Recovery, Inc. for 300 boxes of Narcan at $22 a box.
- **School bus financing:** interfund capital outlay note up to $1,065,332 for seven buses, 12–0. It is a loan from the Education Debt Service Fund to the Education Capital Projects Fund at 0% interest, due within three fiscal years.
- **New court fee:** a $12.50 charge on every misdemeanor and felony cost bill under T.C.A. § 40-3-106, 12–0.
- FY 2026–27 Appropriations Document revised: Trustee's Commission in General Debt Service raised from $70,000 to $80,000. Total of all funds is $130,594,627.10; General Fund is $38,076,361.08. The resolution states this does not affect the tax levy.
- Copier contract for the new jail (BBE Better Business Solutions): $8,250 for three machines plus a $330.78 service payment, 12-month term, 12–0.
- Two Sheriff's vehicles (2017 F-150, 2012 Tahoe) declared surplus and moved to the Highway Department.
- **Library projects:** Library board member Bob Perry said the proposed North and South library projects are estimated at about **$10.5 million combined**. He is seeking state and federal grants and private donations and said a county commitment would help secure them. Commissioners raised concerns about cost, site conditions, and funding. A motion to bring the existing libraries up to specification before starting a new one was made, then withdrawn. Funding discussion was deferred to the September workshop.
- Veterans Service Office remodel: request of $1,397.55 amended up to $5,000, passed 10–2 (Anderson and Hudspeth no). Emergency Operations Center remodel up to $35,123, 12–0. $166,213.63 designated to the Veterans Park Fund Reserve, 12–0.
- A resolution asking elected officials to copy all commissioners on emails about commission business was denied. The County Attorney said it would be non-binding because the Commission has no authority over other elected officials. Noe, Williamson, and Hedgepath voted against denying it.
- Dry Creek bridge on Sams Creek Road named the "Big Donnie Bruce Bridge."
- A motion to keep sending the sports gaming tax to the Highway Fund every year was withdrawn. The County Attorney said it must be voted on yearly.
- Road and Bridge Committee denied raising the Chapmansboro Road speed limit from 30 to 40 mph.
- Chairman Williamson asked the Beer Board to look at lowering the required distance between beer sellers and schools, churches, and public gathering places. No action taken.
- School Board report: groundbreakings for the Harpeth High construction building (Aug 27) and the Maintenance Technology Building (Sept 3).

**Policy and new changes — what they do and who is affected:**
- **$12.50 court charge.** Added to the cost bill in every misdemeanor and felony case in the county. The money goes to the District Attorney's office for the 23rd Judicial District, to be spent "at the sole discretion of the district attorney general." It is on top of an existing $12.50 charge for the public defender. Affected: criminal defendants. This is a fee, not a property tax.
- **School bus note.** New debt of up to $1,065,332. The resolution pledges the county's full faith, credit, and taxing power and authorizes a special tax if needed to repay it. That is standard wording for a Tennessee capital outlay note, and the lender is the county's own education debt fund. Affected: county taxpayers, as the backstop. Not a tax increase by itself.

**Flags / issues:**
- **Library spending is ahead of the decision.** July approved $177,012.50 in architect fees for existing library renovations. August approved $322,875 in architect fees for the new South library. That is **$499,887.50** in architect fees in two months. The same night the $322,875 passed 12–0, commissioners questioned the $10.5 million cost, floated fixing existing libraries first, and deferred the funding discussion. The design money is committed before the commission has agreed to build.
- **Two dates for the same committee vote.** The minutes list the Budget Committee vote on the $322,875 as 8/10/2026 (4–1). The attached resolution lists it as 7/13/2026 (4–1).
- **Finance leadership is leaving all at once.** The County Mayor, the Director of Accounts, the Assistant Director of Accounts, and three commissioners all leave in the same window, at the start of the fiscal year and before the audit. No reasons are recorded. Why both finance leaders resigned together needs corroboration from outside the minutes.
- **Two budget corrections in the first full meeting after adoption.** Two payroll lines were budgeted at the wrong salaries ($13,478), and the Appropriations Document was $10,000 out of balance with the Budget Document. Both are small. Both are errors in a budget adopted six weeks earlier.
- **Vote count error in the minutes.** The body of the minutes records both Veterans Service Office votes as "10 Yes 0 No 2 Absent." The vote tables and the signed resolutions show 10 Yes, 2 No, 0 Absent. All 12 were present.
- **Wrong company named in the copier contract addendum.** The vendor is Better Business Equipment Co. The Iran Divestment and no-boycott-of-Israel clauses say "Trinity certifies." It reads as text copied from another contract. As written, the actual vendor certifies nothing in those two clauses.
- **Agenda items missing from the minutes.** The agenda listed two informational school debt notes ($1,355,000 and $7,000,000) and a $975,000 SRO grant already signed by the Mayor. None appear in the minutes. About $8.4 million of school debt was on the agenda with no record of what was said.
- **The $10 bus mismatch stands.** The Commission approved $1,065,332.00 and cites a 6–0 School Board vote on 8/4. The Board of Education's own line item reads $1,065,322.00.
- **Signature.** The minutes are signed as "Legislative Body Chairman" by what reads as Diana Lovell. Tim Williamson chaired the meeting. A change of chair with the new term is the likely reason, but it is not confirmed here.
- Hustle Recovery, Inc. receives $6,600 with no description of the organization in the record.
- Name errors in the resolutions ("Ms. James Hedgepath," "Randy Now," "Jimmy Hedgepath"). Minor, but these are the signed legal records.

The board's own minutes contain both figures for the same request, $10 apart. The higher figure ($1,065,332.00) matches the County Commission's August 17 agenda for the same seven-bus purchase — so the mismatch flagged last month between the two governments' documents traces back to an inconsistency already present within the Board of Education's own minutes, not two independently-typo'd documents.

A unanimous "yes" roll call paired with a "failed" result is a direct contradiction in the document as written — worth asking the board secretary to clarify which is correct.

  - **Interscholastic Athletics (4.301)** and **Questioning Students and Searches (6.303)** both add identical new language defining sex as "the immutable characteristics of the person's reproductive system that identify the person as male or female, as determined by anatomy and genetics existing at the time of birth," tying athletic eligibility to a student's original birth certificate.
  - **Application and Employment (5.106)** adds an E-Verify requirement for all new hires and removes a prior reference to the Immigration Reform and Control Act of 1986.
  - **Attendance (6.200)** removes the prior "Military Service of Parent/Guardian" section, which had guaranteed a one-day excused absence before/after a parent's deployment and up to 10 excused absences per year to visit a deployed parent — this appears to be a real rollback of a benefit for military families, not a wording cleanup, though no reason is given in the document.
  - Two brand-new policies were adopted: **4.215 Instructional Use of Digital Devices** (limits digital-device use in K-5 classrooms, requires in-person teacher-led instruction as the primary mode) and **6.4053 Outside Applied Behavior Analysis Therapy** (governs private ABA providers working with students on campus).
  - **Student Assignment (6.205)** adds a requirement to separate an alleged child-sexual-abuse victim from an alleged perpetrator within the same school, where substantiated by DCS, a court, or criminal charges.

**August 27, 2026 (Work session):** Still agenda-only, no minutes found. Planned discussion covered insurance premiums and grant carryovers (Title I-A/I-D/II-A/III/IV, IDEA B/PS, a $250,000 safety-camera Ed Debt item, and several six-figure carryover balances), plus Strategic Plan, CCCHS Track, custodial, and ADM/virtual-enrollment updates.

### Kingston Springs — Board of Commissioners

**Source:** approved minutes, August 20, 2026. Pulled 2026-10-07. This replaces the agenda-only write-up from the September pull.

**What happened:**
- **Data center moratorium is now law.** Ordinance 26-006 passed second reading by roll call, "all voting yes." It stops the town from accepting or approving land use, zoning, building, site plan, or conditional use applications for "High Resource Usage Facilities" for 12 months (cut from 18 at first reading) while impacts are studied. No one spoke at the public hearing.
- Ordinance 26-007 passed 4–1 (Coldwell no). It adds a new zoning district, Transitional Mixed-Use Planned Unit Resort Development (TMU-PURD).
- Resolution 26-009 passed, all yes. It updates the personnel policy: changes how much compensatory time can build up and adds a probationary period for new hires.
- FEMA Letter of Map Revision adopted, all yes. Some flood areas change from Zone A to Zone AE, which adds base flood elevations.
- Surplus approved: 2018 Tahoe (Police) and 2000 F-250 (Fire).
- **Senior property tax relief** discussed, no action. The board talked about age and income limits, whether to include veterans, and the effect on town revenue. They cited Burns and Dickson as examples and agreed to gather more information.
- Staff reports: 26 volunteer firefighters; new F-250 in service in November; radios ordered for a January switchover; police chief at a conference on license plate reader cameras; manhole project mostly complete; lagoon liner project ongoing; June financial reports late.
- Resident Bob Sanders (225 Woodlands Drive) asked for tornado sirens and supported a senior tax freeze.

**Policy and new changes — what they do and who is affected:**
- **Ordinance 26-006 (moratorium).** Affected: any developer of a data center or similar heavy power and water user, and landowners who would sell to one. Exemptions exist but are not described in the minutes.
- **Ordinance 26-007 (resort district).** Sets a 100-foot building face limit and a land split of 25% residential, 25% commercial, 50% open space. Open space can include trails and tennis courts. Affected: owners of land that could be rezoned to it, and neighbors.
- **Resolution 26-009.** Affected: town employees, mainly new hires.
- **Letter of Map Revision.** Affected: owners in the remapped flood areas. Staff said it will not change insurance. That claim is not backed by anything in the record.
- **Senior tax relief (discussion only).** If adopted, qualifying seniors pay less and the cost shifts to other taxpayers or to the budget. No numbers were recorded.

**Flags / issues:**
- **The City Attorney is marked absent, then speaks.** Roll call lists City Attorney Tim Potter as "Absent." The minutes then say he "discussed the Town may need to ask for an extension with the TDEC for the Director's Order." Both cannot be right.
- **A state enforcement order is in play.** A TDEC Director's Order (a compliance order from the state environment department) applies to the sewer system, and the town may need more time. Commissioner Stohler asked if the lagoon project could be dropped. The City Manager said TDEC required it. No cost, deadline, or penalty is in the minutes.
- **The resort district ordinance passed on what looks like one reading.** The minutes record a single approval vote and do not label it first or second reading. One commissioner voted no with no reason recorded.
- **June financial reports had not been issued by August 20.** The Finance Director cited software problems and a vacant position. The books for the year that ended June 30 were still open.
- The roll call table leaves Commissioner Stohler, the Public Works Director, and the Fire Chief blank. All three take part in the meeting.
- Todd Verhoven is "Mayor" throughout but "Commissioner" in the roll call.
- "Bob Sanders" spoke here as a Kingston Springs resident. Pegram's Vice Mayor is also named Bob Sanders. Not confirmed to be the same person.
- Other August Kingston Springs minutes (Planning Commission, Design Review Committee, Beer Board) are still not posted.

### Cross-board notes

- **Dollar mismatch on the school-bus financing, now traced to its source:** The Cheatham County Commission's August 17 agenda lists an Interfund Capital Outlay Note "not to exceed $1,065,332.00" for seven school buses. The Board of Education's own August 4 minutes (obtained 2026-09-11) show both $1,065,332.00 (in the motion to revise the agenda) and $1,065,322.00 (in the actual line item and final approval) for the same request — see Board of Education section. The County Commission's figure matches the higher of the two BOE numbers, meaning the $10 discrepancy originates inside the Board of Education's own minutes, not from two separately-typo'd government documents.
- **Harpeth High School construction ties county and town government together:** The Cheatham County Commission's August 17 agenda includes an informational item for a debt note up to $7,000,000 for a "Maintenance/Tech Building and HHS Construction Technical Career Path Building." The same month, both the Kingston Springs Design Review Committee and the Kingston Springs Planning Commission (both meeting August 13) have agenda items reviewing site plans and building materials for the "Harpeth High School CTE (Career and Technical Education) Training Building" at 170 E Kingston Springs Rd. These appear to be the same school construction project moving through county financing and town land-use review in the same month — worth tracking as one story across both jurisdictions once all the relevant minutes are posted.
- **Ashland City's "Parks Board" thread:** Ashland City Council added a "Parks Board Discussion" to its August 4 agenda, then deferred it without discussion on August 11. The Ashland City Parks Advisory Board's own August 25 agenda separately lists a "Parks Master Plan" as new business. It's not confirmed from these documents whether the two are the same underlying issue, but the timing and subject overlap are worth watching together.
- **Kingston Springs Design Review Committee and Planning Commission met the same day (August 13) with a coordinated agenda** — the Planning Commission's agenda explicitly plans to recess for the Design Review Committee's recommendation on the Harpeth High School project, then reconvene. This is a normal, disclosed procedural link between the two bodies, not a red flag on its own — noted here because it's the clearest example this month of two boards' business being directly linked.
- **Transparency gap, updated 2026-10-07:** Three of the eight agenda-only meetings from the September pull now have approved minutes on file: County Commission (Aug 17), Election Commission (Aug 20), and Kingston Springs Board of Commissioners (Aug 20). Still not posted: Board of Education work session (Aug 27), Kingston Springs Planning Commission and Design Review Committee (Aug 13), Kingston Springs Beer Board (Aug 20), Pleasant View Parks and Recreation (Aug 24), and Pleasant View Beer Board (Aug 27).
