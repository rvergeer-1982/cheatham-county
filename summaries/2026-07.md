# July 2026 — Meeting Summary

### Cheatham County — County Commission (July 20, 2026)

**What happened:**
- Naming of the new jail: a motion to defer naming until September **failed** 6–5 (1 abstain). The original motion — to name the facility after former Sheriff Doris Weakley — then **passed** 9–2 (1 abstain, Randy Noe). Mike Breedlove and Diana Pike Lovell voted No; Breedlove argued the facility should stay "Cheatham County Justice Center" out of respect for fallen deputies rather than one individual.
- Four zoning map changes (Proffitt, Schoeneck, Brasher, C. Williams) were all approved 12–0 with no public comment for or against.
- Multiple County General Fund, Highway/Public Works, Drug Control, and Solid Waste budget amendments were approved, including a $270,974.26 transfer for building/contents insurance because the FY26-27 budgeted amount didn't cover the new jail facility (invoice arrived after the budget was already set), and a $30,560 transfer from an "Unused Jailer Salary" reserve to buy new Sheriff's Department communication equipment.
- A $322,875 line item for Social, Cultural, and Recreation Projects was deferred to the August meeting (11–1); a separate $177,012.50 line for the same category (architect fees for existing Cheatham County Library renovations) was kept and approved.
- Two interfund capital outlay notes for schools were approved 9–3: up to $1,355,000 for instructional supplies/equipment/vehicles, and up to $7,000,000 for the Maintenance/Tech Building and HHS Construction Technical Career Path Building. Both matched votes the School Board had already taken (6–0) on July 9.
- Mayor's signature approved on a contract with Madison County Juvenile Detention Center ($200/day per juvenile, July 2026–June 2027).
- A resolution recognized Quinten Gordan for 40 years as custodian at Pegram Elementary School.

**Flags / issues:**
- Outgoing 1st District School Board member John Patrick told the Commission he'd "observed no evidence of fraud or lack of transparency" during his time on the School Board. This is a self-assessment recorded in the minutes with no independent corroboration — noted, not verified.
- The insurance-cost overrun ($270,974.26, tied to the new jail) and the jailer-salary-to-equipment reallocation ($30,560) are both administrative but represent real money moving after the fact; worth watching for a pattern in future months.
- Randy Noe distributed a resolution asking that all commissioners be copied on emails about pending county business — an unresolved process complaint, to be addressed at the August workshop.
- The budget document lists "Social, Cultural, and Recreation Projects" twice with two different dollar figures ($322,875, deferred, and $177,012.50, approved) — not a contradiction once read closely, but a likely source of public confusion.

### Cheatham County — Planning Commission (July 2, 2026)

**What happened:**
- Approved a site plan for existing commercial/storage buildings on Dorris Winters Rd. (Sheron), 6–0 roll call, after staff flagged unresolved questions about landscape screening extent and parking classification for "construction sales and service" use (not specifically defined in county parking regs).
- Approved a zone change (E-1 to R-1) for a Split Rail Circle parcel, 6–0, to allow the owners to eventually split the lot — the application was signed by only one of the two listed property owners.
- Continued (deferred) discussion of a new C-5 Neighborhood Commercial Mixed Use zoning district to the next meeting, including definitions for sober-living/substance-rehab facility categories.
- Continued (deferred) discussion of data center regulation — current zoning treats data centers as "Extensive Manufacturing," allowed by right in I-1/I-2 districts with no development standards; staff presented buffering/design-standard options from other jurisdictions for future consideration.
- During public forum, resident Linda Ryder urged the county to add restrictions on data centers, calling the county's current lack of regulation a liability.

**Flags / issues:**
- The zone-change application signed by only one of two co-owners was approved anyway — worth confirming this doesn't create a legal question later.
- Both the data-center and C-5 zoning items were tabled for a second time (per the "Continued from June 4" notations) — a recurring topic that keeps getting pushed to the next meeting.

### Cheatham County — Board of Education

**Update 2026-09-11:** The July 9 minutes were originally unreadable (broken BOEconnect viewer file); a real PDF has since been obtained and is summarized below.

**July 9, 2026 (Regular meeting) — now readable:**
- Chairman Risner reviewed Director Stacy Brown's annual evaluation: a cumulative score of **4.24 out of 5** ("exceeded expectations"), based on individual board-member scoring sheets (attached to the minutes) covering board relations, job performance, and ethics. Scores were not unanimous — Amber Locke rated several categories "At Expectation" (3s) or even "Below Expectation" (2s on two items) while other members scored heavily in the 4-5 range, meaning the "exceeded expectations" headline score reflects a real spread of opinion, not consensus.
- ACESA's SIP presentation: first year the school earned "Reward School" status.
- Cal Blacker, Director of Transportation and Maintenance, gave the Five Year Plan update: roofing at Sycamore HS and Harpeth MS delayed by 22 rain days in May/June; district-wide HVAC replacement (534 PTAC units) nearly complete; athletic field lighting at Harpeth HS/HMS also behind schedule for the same weather reason.
- Consent agenda approved (6-0): June 4 and June 29 minutes, and disposal of a flood-damaged copier.
- Approved (6-0 each): a $15,000 Preschool Development Grant; the nutrition price increases exactly as listed on the agenda (student lunch $3.75→$4.00, adult lunch $4.75→$6.00, etc. — **this confirms the increase was formally adopted**, not just proposed); and an updated Ed Debt request combining a $250,000 safety reduction, $7,000,000 for the Maintenance/Tech Building and HHS Construction Building, $1,355,000 in capital outlay items (vans, a skid steer, snow plows, computers, sound systems — full itemized list attached), and $300,000 earmarked for FY27/28 SHS field lights.
- The $7,000,000 building figure is backed by actual bids in the attached documents: $4,789,500 (Maintenance/IT Building) + $1,323,445 (HHS Construction Building), each plus a 15% contingency, totaling $7,029,886.75 — slightly over the $7,000,000 board-approved figure.

**July 21, 2026 (Special Called Work Session):** Still agenda-only, no minutes found. Listed policy revisions and two new policies (4.215 Instructional Use of Digital Devices; 6.4053 Outside Applied Behavior Analysis Therapy) ahead of an August 4 vote.

**July 30, 2026 (Work Session):** Still agenda-only, no minutes found. Listed a library update, the $50,000 Perkins Reserve Grant and $30,000 Preschool Access for All Network grant (both later approved August 4 — see next month's summary), a custodial item, and review of the August 4 meeting agenda.

**Flags / issues:**
- Work sessions (July 21, July 30) don't produce approved minutes on BOEconnect — this appears to be structural for this board (work sessions get an agenda only), not a documentation gap specific to July.
- The Director's evaluation score spread (Amber Locke's lower marks vs. other members' near-perfect scores) is worth keeping in view if board/director relations become a story later — a 4.24 average masks real disagreement among individual board members.

### Cross-board notes

- **Data centers / high-resource facilities were a live issue in two separate jurisdictions this same month.** The Cheatham County Planning Commission (July 2) discussed adding development standards for data centers (currently unregulated beyond default "Extensive Manufacturing" zoning), while Kingston Springs' Planning Commission (July 9) and Board of Commissioners (July 16) moved a full moratorium ordinance from proposal to passage in one week — though the Board shortened the moratorium period from the 18 months the Planning Commission recommended to 12 months. Notably, the same resident, **Linda Ryder**, spoke against data centers at *both* the County Commission (July 20) and County Planning Commission (July 2) meetings — consistent, corroborated public advocacy on this issue across bodies.
- **School capital debt notes were coordinated across bodies as designed and are now independently confirmed on both sides:** the School Board's own July 9 minutes (obtained 2026-09-11) show a 6-0 vote for the updated Ed Debt request ($7,000,000 buildings + $1,355,000 capital items + $300,000 field lights + $250,000 safety), and the County Commission's July 20 minutes show the matching $1,355,000 and $7,000,000 interfund capital outlay notes passing 9-3. The two records agree on the dollar figures.
- **Early voting timing lines up across documents:** Pleasant View's July 13 minutes note early voting beginning July 17, consistent with the county's stated reason for the Election Commission not meeting this month.
- **Possible same-person overlap worth double-checking:** A "Jill Niccolich" is listed as present at the Cheatham County Planning Commission meeting (July 2) and is also the Pleasant View Alderman who resigned July 13, citing an employment conflict. We did not independently confirm whether this is the same individual, but if so, her resignation from the Pleasant View board raises the question of whether the same employment conflict affects her county Planning Commission role — worth asking directly rather than assuming either way.
- **The Ellersly subdivision (Kingston Springs) surfaced in three consecutive documents this month** (Planning Commission minutes, Board of Commissioners minutes via the related Riverkeepers matter, and the July 23 workshop agenda) without a clear resolution — a genuinely recurring, cross-meeting problem rather than three unrelated mentions.
- No contradictions were found between what any two boards said about the same factual event (e.g., dollar figures, vote outcomes) — the inconsistencies identified above are internal to single documents (Kingston Springs Board of Commissioners roll call vs. adjournment; Kingston Springs Planning Commission's contradictory community-input note), not disagreements between bodies.
