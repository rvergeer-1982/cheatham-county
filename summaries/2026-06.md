### Cheatham County — County Commission (June 29, 2026)

**What happened**
- Held the public hearing and final vote on the FY2026-2027 county budget and property tax rate. Several residents spoke against a proposed property tax increase, including two who specifically raised concerns about AI/data centers.
- The Commission voted 7-5 to fund two full-time Court Security Officer positions by reallocating money from part-time security and jailer reserve lines (no new tax dollars).
- The Commission then voted 7-5 to zero out the proposed property tax increase entirely by reallocating $1,144,568.64 from the Education Capital Projects Fund balance. This followed discussion of the effect on school maintenance and capital projects.
- A related motion to strip the Education Debt Service section out of the budget and defer it to the July meeting passed 9-3.
- The final amended 2026-2027 Budget/Appropriations/Non-Profit Document passed 8-4 — a closely divided vote on the county's main annual budget.
- The fire tax levy (2026-2027 rates for Cheatham County Fire District and Harpeth Ridge) passed 11-1, with Commissioner Weakley voting no after arguing vacant land shouldn't be taxed at the same fire-tax rate as developed property; the County Attorney said state law doesn't allow differentiated rates.
- A resolution to dissolve the County Archives department and move it under the Public Library failed 5-7 on one version, then a substitute motion to defer the whole question until September passed 9-3. The Library Director testified she'd ask the Library Board to remove Archives from the library building if the move isn't approved.
- Routine items: numerous small budget-amendment resolutions (nearly all 12-0), approval of Motorola radio contract for EMS/EMA/Sheriff, Beacon Technologies fire-alarm monitoring, Subway lease renewal at Sycamore Square, Clarke Power Services generator maintenance, jail-contract change (proceeding with the current contractor except for old-jail evacuation work, "due to inconsistencies with architecture and other issues" — a new architect will be brought in for that piece), and appointment of Lisa Goff to the Library Board of Trustees.

**Flags / issues**
- The budget process was genuinely contentious, not a formality: three separate close votes (7-5, 7-5, 8-4) moved money out of education funds to avoid raising property taxes. Commissioner Blacker said afterward, on the record, that he believed the school system was negatively affected by the funding decisions — this is the commission's own member on record disagreeing with the outcome, not an outside inference.
- The resolution approving the Motorola radio contract (Resolution 20(D)) has a handwritten note on the signed copy reading "Emailed Rachel for contract, isn't in Mayor's agenda" — suggesting the contract document itself was not actually in hand/attached when the vote was certified. Worth confirming the contract terms independently.
- The jail contract change explicitly cites "inconsistencies with architecture and other issues" as the reason for bringing in a new architect/contractor for part of the project — this is a red flag on a public construction project that deserves follow-up on what those inconsistencies were and their cost impact.
- The archives dissolution question flip-flopped within the same meeting (failed, then a differently-worded deferral passed) — process was messy, and the underlying disagreement (library vs. county-department status for Archives) remains unresolved until at least September.
- $22,670.75 was transferred to replenish a county gasoline line after a check was "stolen from USPS mail, altered, and fraudulently cashed" — a fraud incident mentioned only in passing as a budget line item, with no further detail on the county's response or whether this was reported to law enforcement separately.
